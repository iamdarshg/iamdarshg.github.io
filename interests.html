00 Beyond the lab

The things I do away from the workbench—and what they give back to the work.

The Gym Swimming MUNs Gaming
The Gym

Training for a longer day

I started going to the gym because long hours at a screen were making the rest of the day feel smaller. The routine is simple: show up, add a little weight when it makes sense, and keep going when progress is not obvious.

It gives me a useful break from projects. Training is physical, repetitive, and honest. A missed lift does not need a complicated explanation; it just tells me what to work on next.

Swimming

A quieter kind of focus

Swimming is the place where the noise drops away. There is a fixed distance, a repeated stroke, and enough time between lengths to let a problem rearrange itself.

I usually come back from the pool with a clearer next step—not necessarily a solution, but a better question to take back to the desk.

MUNs

Learning to make a case

Model United Nations taught me to prepare properly, listen for the constraint underneath an argument, and explain a position without hiding behind jargon. I took part in Harvard MUN in 2023 and 2024, along with IIT Techfest MUN.

The useful part was not winning every room. It was learning how to turn a complicated position into something another person could actually respond to.

Gaming

Games as a place to experiment

Minecraft was one of the first places I learned to build systems without a worksheet telling me what the answer should look like. You gather materials, test an idea, break something, and try again.

I still play Minecraft, Trackmania, and Overwatch. They are different kinds of play, but all three reward attention: planning a build, finding a faster line, or making a decision under pressure.
